# Biophysical Modelling of Nile Crocodile Thermoregulation

**Research project | BSc (Hons) Zoology | University of Pretoria | 2025**

## Overview

How do environmental conditions and behavioural strategies influence the body temperature of Nile crocodiles (*Crocodylus niloticus*)?

This research evaluated the performance of a biophysical ectotherm model in predicting crocodile body temperatures in the Olifants River Gorge, Kruger National Park, South Africa. The study investigated how temperature extremes, basking behaviour, seasonal variation and body size influence thermoregulation, with particular reference to the historical pansteatitis-associated crocodile mortality events in the Olifants River system.

## Research objectives

- Evaluate model predictions against empirically measured crocodile body temperatures.
- Optimise model parameters using sensitivity analysis.
- Investigate seasonal differences in thermoregulation and basking substrate conditions.
- Compare predicted body temperatures under basking and restricted-basking scenarios.
- Assess whether cold spells could have contributed to crocodile mortality events.

## Methods and tools

- **R:** Statistical computing and model analysis.
- **NicheMapR:** Biophysical modelling of ectotherm thermoregulation and environmental microclimates.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** Assessing how changes in physiological, morphological and behavioural parameters influence model predictions.
- **Model validation:** Comparing simulated and measured temperatures using root mean square error (RMSE), Bland–Altman analysis and Cohen's d.
- **Comparative analysis:** Evaluating seasonal conditions and crocodile size classes.

## Key findings

- **Parameterisation matters:** Species-specific parameter adjustments improved the alignment between simulated and measured body temperatures.
- **Sensitivity analysis improved model performance:** The sensitivity-optimised model achieved a mean bias of −0.5 °C and an RMSE of 6.4 °C.
- **Basking influences thermoregulation:** Restricting basking reduced the model's ability to reproduce observed body temperatures.
- **Seasonal conditions matter:** The model captured seasonal thermoregulatory patterns associated with differences in basking conditions and substrate selection.
- **Cold stress is unlikely to be the sole explanation for mortality:** The findings suggest that thermal constraints may have acted alongside other environmental stressors, including pollution and habitat degradation.

## Conservation significance

This research demonstrates the value of biophysical modelling for investigating the relationships between environmental conditions and ectotherm physiology. Understanding these relationships can contribute to assessing wildlife vulnerability to climatic extremes and informing riverine habitat conservation.
